Pre-Built Cabinet Vault Plan

Buy a well-built cabinet with good bones; spend your effort mastering the seal and the electronics. All 8 instruments hung, no amps. Pairs with guitar-cabinet-plan.html for climate detail.

0. The strategy

The carcass is the hard, skill-heavy part. So instead of building it, we buy a cabinet that already has the two things that are painful to make well — solid wood and a tall, deep, hinged-door enclosure — and we focus on the two things we can master at home: an airtight seal and a reliable climate-control system.

There is a whole purpose-built category for exactly this (humidified guitar display cabinets). You can either buy one turnkey (climate already built in — least work) or retrofit a quality general cabinet (you build the climate system — the "master it yourself" path). This plan covers both and recommends one.
Hard rule learned from the TONSTAD detour: the base cabinet must be solid wood or solid-core plywood with hinged doors. Reject anything with honeycomb/hollow-core panels (nothing to mount hangers or seals to) or sliding doors (cannot be gasketed airtight). See the buyer's checklist in §3.

1. Size target (all 8 hung, no amps)

Dropping the amp zone and hanging everything edge-out sets the minimum interior:

DimensionNeedWhy
Interior height~55"+ clearTallest hang is the 45" Music Man bass + ~3" hanger headroom + ~4" bottom airflow.
Interior depth~20"+ (≥18" min)Acoustic bodies are 14–16" wide; hung edge-out that width runs front-to-back, plus airflow behind.
Interior width~48–60"8 instruments edge-out pack into ~37" minimum, but commercial spacing wants ~6" per neck for easy access.
External height~62–72"Fits any room; far more stable and buildable than the 93" amp version.

So we're shopping for roughly a 50–63" wide × 20–23" deep × ~72" tall solid-wood, hinged-door cabinet.

2. Shortlist of pre-built bases

Tier A — Purpose-built guitar humidor cabinets (turnkey)

Made for this exact job: solid hardwood, glass doors, cork neck rests, and climate control already integrated. Most "good wood + infra" of anything available — you'd mainly be verifying/upgrading the electronics, not building them.

Trade-off: these are premium (roughly $3,000–9,000+ depending on size/wood — confirm current pricing). And because climate is already built in, there's less for you to "master." Best if you value turnkey + heirloom furniture over the project.

Tier B — Quality general display cabinet, retrofitted (recommended)

A solid-wood curio / display / barrister cabinet or lighted china cabinet with hinged glass doors. You add the full climate system — this is the "good bones, we master the seal & electronics" sweet spot, at a fraction of Tier A cost.

  • Look for makers like Howard Miller, Pulaski, Hooker, or solid-wood Amish/used pieces. Search terms: “solid wood lighted curio cabinet hinged glass doors 72 inch,” “display cabinet solid wood glass door.”
  • Many curios are too shallow (~12") — insist on ≥18" depth (often called “deep” curios or display/gun-style cabinets). This is the #1 thing to filter on.
  • Typical cost $500–1,500 new, less used.

Tier C — Solid-wood wardrobe / armoire, retrofitted (budget)

A solid-wood wardrobe/armoire with hinged doors. Cheapest pre-built bones and the easiest to seal (solid doors gasket beautifully), but no glass — no display. Add a glass insert if you want to see in. Good if storage/protection matters more than showing the collection.

A widely shared IKEA-hack guitar humidor exists too — useful as a worked example of sealing + Boveda, but it relies on particleboard, so treat it as inspiration rather than the recommended base.

4. Retrofit — the seal (your job #1)

  1. Gasket the doors. Adhesive EPDM or silicone weatherstrip around each door opening; add compression/draw latches or roller catches so the door clamps the gasket. (Avoid cheap open-cell foam — it off-gasses.)
  2. Seal the seams. Run paintable silicone on every interior joint — panel-to-panel and around the back. Most furniture is “dust-tight,” not air-tight; the seal is your work.
  3. Seal a cable pass-through. Drill one grommet hole low in the back, fit a grommet, caulk it, and use a drip loop on every cord.
  4. Tame the wood. If raw interior wood, a coat of low-VOC water-based poly or a Spanish-cedar liner buffers humidity and (for veneer-over-MDF) blocks off-gassing. Let any finish cure fully before loading.
  5. Anti-tip. Strap/anchor to wall studs.
Verify the seal before trusting it: close it up empty, run the system, and watch that RH holds against the room for a few days.

5. Retrofit — the electronics (your job #2)

This is the same control system as the master plan (guitar-cabinet-plan.html) — reuse it verbatim; only the box changed.

  • Humidifier (ultrasonic, distilled) + dehumidifier (Peltier or small compressor) inside, on the floor, mist aimed away from instruments.
  • Two controllers (e.g., Inkbird IHC-200 humidity, ITC-308 temp) mounted outside the sealed space, with probes through the grommet. Give them a dead-band between humidify/dehumidify setpoints so they don't fight.
  • Two small circulation fans (120 mm USB) for even RH; one low, one high.
  • Independent logging sensor (salt-calibrated) with high/low alarms — never trust a single device.
  • GFCI surge strip as the single mains hub; one cord leaves the cabinet via a drip loop.
  • Target 45–50% RH, 68–75°F. Real temperature control mostly comes from the room's HVAC.
If you buy a Tier A cabinet, humidification is already integrated — your electronics work shrinks to adding what it lacks (often dehumidification, circulation fans, and an independent logging sensor/alarm).

6. Hanging 8 edge-out

  • Mount a solid hardwood/plywood hanger board across the top, anchored into the cabinet's solid frame members — never into a thin back or hollow panel.
  • Use cork/felt-lined neck yokes (the Tier A cabinets use cork rests + adjustable rails — copy that).
  • Hang edge-out (profile toward the glass) so each instrument uses only its body depth of width; stagger two heights for finger room and a clear view.
  • Nitro-finish caution: ensure rests and any foam/rubber are nitrocellulose-safe (or cork) — the wrong material can mar vintage finishes over time.

7. Recommendation

If you want to master the seal + electronics yourself (the project): go Tier B — a solid-wood, ≥18"-deep, ~72" lighted display/curio cabinet with hinged glass doors, and build the full climate system into it. Best balance of good bones, display, cost, and hands-on control.

If you want it done right with minimal build effort and the budget is there: buy a Tier A ClimaCab Grand or Guitar Habitat (Large) — they already nail the wood, hanging infra, and base climate; you just add dehumidification + logging.

Either way, the design targets are the same: solid wood, hinged gasketed doors, ~55"+ clear height, ~20"+ depth, 45–50% RH with a dead-band, and an independent alarm.
