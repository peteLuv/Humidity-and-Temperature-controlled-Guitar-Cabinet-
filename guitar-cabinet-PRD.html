1 · Overview & Goals

Store and display a collection of 8 instruments in a stable, controlled micro-climate while keeping two combo amps and a pedalboard close at hand — in a single floor-standing cabinet that fits a normal room.

Problem

Instruments — especially acoustics with nitrocellulose finishes — are damaged by low humidity (cracking, fret sprout), high humidity (swelling, glue failure, finish hazing), and rapid swings. A normal cabinet does nothing to regulate this. The amps generate heat and must not share sealed air with the instruments.

Solution

A two-zone cabinet: a sealed, cedar-lined upper vault holding all 8 instruments at 45–50% RH, sitting above an open, vented lower amp zone that lets amp heat escape to the room. A single smart controller manages humidity and air circulation and raises alarms; temperature is left to the room (which already sits in range) and watched, not actively driven.

Goals & non-goals

Goals
  • Hold vault at 45–50% RH, ±5%, year-round.
  • Damp temperature swings; keep within a safe band.
  • Display all 8 instruments, visible behind glass.
  • Instrument-safe materials and air (low off-gassing).
  • Remote visibility + alerts when out of band.
  • Fit under an 86.3″ / 220 cm ceiling; be tip-safe.
Non-goals
  • Active cooling — impractical in a sealed wooden box; relies on room ambient.
  • Climate control of the amp zone — deliberately open/vented.
  • Storing the 4 electrics' cases inside — hard cases live elsewhere.
  • A furniture-grade finish spec — covered separately.

Why this is the right call (pros)
  • Controls the variable that actually matters — humidity — accurately, with a single-brain deadband (no fighting).
  • Remote alarms + logging: you're told before damage happens.
  • Instrument-safe throughout: Baltic birch, Spanish cedar, low-VOC finish, neutral-cure silicone, distilled-water mist.
  • Display-grade and safe: tempered glass, anchored anti-tip, GFCI power.
  • Consolidated wiring; fewer boxes than a multi-Inkbird rig.
Trade-offs (cons) & mitigations
  • DIY effort/skill, esp. the airtight door → patient gasket fit + leak check.
  • Up-front cost (~$2,100 + glass) → most is the shell + glass, both one-time.
  • App/firmware dependency for logging → independent SensorPush-type monitor as a second, separate alarm; cedar buffers passively if power drops.
  • No active cooling → acceptable indoors; high-temp alarm + room AC cover the hot edge.
Critical requirement: the door seal is the make-or-break. If it leaks, the controllers chase the room all day and never hold setpoint. Budget time for gasket fit, latch tension, and an empty leak/burn-in test before any instrument goes in.

4 · System Architecture

Labeled system map (from the interactive 3D model). Sealed vault on top; open amp zone below; all climate & electrical gear lives in the vented amp zone, outside the sealed air, with sensing probes passing up through a single sealed grommet.

Operating principle

  • Two thermally/▒air-separated zones. The ¾″ divider is caulked airtight — it is the vault floor. Everything below it is open to the room.
  • Gear stays out of the sealed air. Controller, humidifier, dehumidifier, power strip all sit in the amp zone; only thin probes and mist/air routes cross the divider through the sealed grommet (with a drip loop).
  • Closed-loop humidity. The controller reads vault RH and switches the humidifier or dehumidifier outlet to hold the band, while the two fans keep the vault air mixed.
  • Temperature is observed, not driven. A high/low alarm is the only temperature action.
  • Independent watchdog. A separate battery monitor logs RH/temp and alarms even if the main controller fails.

7 · Build Sequence

  1. Confirm & order. Measure real plywood thickness; hand the cut list to the dealer (incl. the Ø2″ divider hole). Order cherry. Hold the glass order until the door is built.
  2. Pre-finish vault interior. Sand and apply 2 coats low-VOC water-based poly to inner faces you won't cedar-line; let cure fully (it off-gasses).
  3. Assemble carcass. Bottom, then divider at the 25.5″ line, then top, housed between the full-height sides; glue + pocket screws; check square; glue + screw the back.
  4. Sealed pass-through. Fit the grommet in the divider hole; bead caulk around it; this is the only route into the vault.
  5. Caulk the vault airtight. Every interior seam above the divider. Leave the amp zone open — never seal it.
  6. Line the vault with cedar. Walls + back + floor; brad nails / adhesive; leave the door opening clear.
  7. Hanger board + hangers. Mount F to the back; fit the 8 hangers to the measured layout (edge-out, height-staggered).
  8. Build & hang the door. Cherry frame with a rabbet; set glass on glazing tape + a neutral-cure silicone bead; hang on the piano hinge; add the EPDM gasket and 3 draw latches so closing compresses the seal.
  9. Install gear. Controller, humidifier, dehumidifier, GFCI strip in the amp zone; fans + sensor in the vault; route probes through the grommet with drip loops.
  10. Anchor anti-tip to wall studs.
  11. Burn-in & calibrate. Run empty 2–3 days; salt-calibrate the sensor; set the RH band + deadband; leak-check the door; confirm it holds 45–50% RH before instruments go in.

8 · Climate & Control Logic

ConditionActionDevice
Vault RH < 45% (low edge)Energize humidifier outlet; run fansHumidifier (cool-mist) + fans
Vault RH > 50% (high edge)Energize dehumidifier outlet; run fansMini dehumidifier + fans
RH inside bandBoth moisture devices off; fans cycle for mixingFans only
Temp < 15 °C or > 25 °CAlarm (app + monitor). No active heat/cool.Controller + remote monitor
RH out of band > set timePush alarm; log eventController + remote monitor
Power lossCedar buffers passively; monitor (battery) keeps logging/alarmingCedar + independent monitor
Why cool-mist: warm-mist humidifiers are essentially small heaters (~200–400 W) and would couple humidity to temperature in this small box. Cool-mist keeps the two independent — humidity from the humidifier, temperature left to the room. Use distilled water to avoid mineral dust on finishes.
Deadband rule: separate the humidify and dehumidify setpoints (e.g. on at 44% / off at 47% for humidify; on at 51% / off at 48% for dehumidify) so the two devices can never run against each other.

Open items

  • Confirm ceiling height is a true 86.3″ after molding/baseboard, leaving room to tilt the cabinet upright.
  • Measure instruments (headstock widths, longest body) to finalize the 8-up hanging layout before cutting.
  • Combo humidify/dehumidify unit? If a single self-regulating combo is chosen, it manages RH itself and the 79 reduces to fans + monitoring — confirm it does both automatically with an accurate humidistat.
  • Order glass last — only after the cherry door is built and the opening measured (tempered can't be trimmed).
  • Confirm Controller 79 outlet count (≥2 switchable) and that it has a humidity trigger.
