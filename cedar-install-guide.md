# Spanish Cedar Lining — Installation Guide

Step-by-step for installing the pre-cut ¼" Spanish cedar in the sealed vault
(interior **97 × 46 × 125 cm = 38.2 × 18.1 × 49.2 in**). You have the 51-piece
order from `cedar-order-email.md`. **The divider isn't fixed yet — use that.**

> **Golden rule:** the cedar should end up **bare, snug but not jammed, and able to
> move a hair.** It's a humidity buffer, not a structural glue-down. Soft, forgiving
> material — every trim below takes seconds with a block plane or one saw pass.

---

## What each pile of planks is for

| Pieces | Size | Face | How they run | Rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 × full | 5" × 38⅛" | **Back** (9) + **Floor** (3) | left ↔ right | — |
| 28 × full | 5" × 18⅛" | **Sides** (9+9) + **Ceiling** (7) | front ↔ back | — |
| 2 | 4¼" × 38⅛" | Back **top** row | left ↔ right | last row |
| 3 | 4¼" × 18⅛" | Side **top** rows | front ↔ back | last row |
| 2 | 3⅛" × 38⅛" | Floor **back** edge | left ↔ right | last row |
| 2 | 3¼" × 18⅛" | Ceiling **side** edge | front ↔ back | last row |

Each wall is **9 full rows (45") + one narrow top row (4¼") = 49¼"**. The narrow
strips always go in the **least-visible spot**: tops of walls, back of floor, one
side of the ceiling. (Spares included — you have ~10% extra, so you can afford a
botched cut.)

---

## STEP 0 — Acclimate (do this the day it arrives; don't skip)

Lay the planks **flat, loosely stacked with air gaps**, in the room where the
cabinet lives, for **4–7 days**. Cedar shipped from a different climate will shrink
or cup after you install it if you skip this — that's where gaps come from. Don't
install cedar straight out of the box.

---

## STEP 1 — Dry-fit and set the divider to the planks

This is the payoff for the divider being loose.

1. **Sort** the planks into 5 stacks (back / 2 sides / floor / ceiling) per the table.
2. **Use the planks as your height gauge.** Stand a dry column against the back wall:
   **9 full planks + 1 narrow (4¼") strip**, edges touching, no gaps. That stack
   *is* your exact vault height (~49¼").
3. **Set the divider** so the gap from its top face to the vault ceiling equals that
   stack — snug, the ceiling just kissing the top of the column. Nudge it a few mm
   until it matches, then mark its position. (Target: divider top ≈ **65 cm** from the
   cabinet floor, vault ≈ **125 cm** — but trust the plank stack over the number.)
4. **Check the width:** lay a 38⅛" back plank across — it should drop in with a
   hairline of clearance at each end. Good.
5. **Fix the divider** at that height (glue + screws per `structural-review.md`,
   incl. the stiffener rail). It needs to be solid before you line on top of it.

> Don't line the side walls *before* the divider goes in — once the sides are lined,
> the cedar narrows the opening and the 97 cm divider won't pass it. Divider **first**.

---

## STEP 2 — Floor (easiest first; can be done on the bench)

Floor planks run **left ↔ right**, stacked front-to-back.

1. Lay the **3 full planks (38⅛")** on the divider top, starting at the **front**,
   working back. Snug them edge to edge.
2. The **back edge** uses the narrow **3⅛" strip** — measure the actual remaining gap
   to the back wall and trim the strip to suit.
3. **Cut the Ø5 cm cable hole**: find where the divider's hole is (6 cm from the back,
   centered), and bore a matching 2" hole through the cedar above it with a hole saw.
4. Fasten (see **Fastening** below).

> Tip: you *can* line the floor with the divider out on a bench (much comfier), then
> install the divider with the cedar already on it — the cedar sits on top, so it
> doesn't affect the divider fitting into the 97 cm opening.

---

## STEP 3 — Back wall (full width, the reference for the corners)

Back planks run **left ↔ right**, full **38⅛"** width, stacked bottom to top.

1. Start at the **bottom**, resting the first plank on the floor cedar's back edge.
2. Stack **9 full rows**, each a single full-width plank (no end seams — that's why
   there's no brick pattern). Keep them level; a pencil line or a spacer helps.
3. **Top row** = the **4¼" strip**. Measure the actual remaining gap to the ceiling
   and trim the strip to fit — **the top row absorbs all the accumulated tolerance.**

---

## STEP 4 — Side walls (these get the corner trim)

Side planks run **front ↔ back** (18⅛"), stacked bottom to top, 9 + top strip each.

**The corner rule:** the back wall cedar is now ¼" (6 mm) proud of the bare back wall.
So each side plank's **back end must lose ~¼"** to butt cleanly against the back
cedar instead of the bare wall. Trim ~¼" off the **back end** of each side plank
(down to ~17⅞"). The **front end stays as-is** — it's the open door edge.

1. Bottom plank rests on the floor cedar, back end butting the back cedar.
2. Stack **9 rows**; **top row** = the **4¼" strip**, trimmed to the real gap.
3. Repeat for the other side.

> Same rule everywhere: **whichever face goes in first stays full; the face that
> butts into it loses ¼" at the butting end.** Back is full → sides trim to it.

---

## STEP 5 — Ceiling (overhead — adhesive + brads + a prop)

Ceiling planks run **front ↔ back** (18⅛"), stacked left to right, 7 + side strip.
Same corner rule: trim each plank's **back end ~¼"** to butt the back cedar.

1. Work from one side across. Each plank: a few dabs of adhesive **+ 2–3 brads** to
   hold it up, then **wedge a prop** (a stick cut a touch long, or a padded board on a
   spreader) against the floor to clamp it while the adhesive grabs. Painter's tape
   helps too.
2. The far **side edge** = the **3¼" strip**, trimmed to the real gap.

---

## Fastening (instrument-safe) — brad nailer + spot glue

Brad nailer does the holding; the glue is just a few dabs. **Do NOT glue the whole
back of each plank** — right instinct.

- **Glue: a few dabs only.** Pea-sized dabs of PVA wood glue (Titebond) or a low-VOC
  construction adhesive, roughly **every 8–12"** — so **3–4 dabs on a 38" back plank,
  2 on an 18" side plank.** Never a full bead or full coverage: solid cedar swells at
  50% RH and gluing it down solid can cup or crack it. The dabs just stop rattle/lift;
  the brads clamp.
- **Brads: 18-gauge** (or 23-ga pins for near-invisible holes). **Length by panel:**
  - **Back wall: ⅝" (16 mm)** — the back panel is only ½" (13 mm); longer can poke
    through to the outside.
  - **Sides / floor / ceiling: up to ¾" (19 mm)** — those are ¾" ply, no poke-through.
  - **2–3 brads per plank end + one mid-span**, placed near the top edge/ends where the
    next row or the cherry face frame hides them.
- **Cheap gun is fine** (the glue does the real work). First thing: **test-fire on a
  cedar offcut and dial the depth/pressure down** — soft cedar craters if over-driven.
  Flush or a hair proud is right; since glue holds, any bad brad pulls out after cure.
- **Avoid** contact cement / aromatic solvent adhesives (VOCs in a sealed box). Off-gas
  the vault open **1–2 weeks** before instruments regardless.
- **Ceiling** leans hardest on the nailer (gravity) — brads + dabs + a prop while it grabs.

**Home Depot list:** one 18-gauge brad nailer (pneumatic if you have a compressor, else
a cheap electric/cordless) + a pack each of **⅝"** and **¾"** 18-ga brads.

---

## Spacing, gaps, and finish

- **Install snug but not forced.** The planks are at room humidity now; if you jam
  them bone-tight, they'll buckle when they swell at 50% RH. Edge-to-edge contact with
  no force is right; a visible **0.5 mm** breathing gap is fine and invisible.
- **Push tolerance to the last (narrow) row** of every face — that's why those strips
  are sized to be trimmed on site.
- **Leave the cedar bare.** No oil, no shellac, no wax — a finish kills the humidity
  buffering and the smell. The exterior shellac (plan §"Exterior finish") never touches
  the inside.
- **Handle clean** — skin oils mark raw cedar; wash hands or wear nitrile gloves.

---

## Front edges & the cherry face frame

Don't fuss over the **front-most ends** of the planks. The **cherry face frame
overhangs the opening ~2 cm** and forms the gasket lip, so it **covers the front edge
of the cedar** all the way around. Just keep the cedar from standing proud of where
the door gasket will seal — flush or a hair shy of the front plane.

---

## Quick tool/material checklist

- Pin or brad nailer + ⅝–1" pins/brads (or a tack hammer + fine panel pins)
- Titebond or low-VOC construction adhesive
- Fine pull saw / block plane / track saw for the ¼" corner trims; 120–150 grit sanding block
- 2" hole saw (cable hole)
- Tape measure, square, pencil; shims; painter's tape; a prop stick for the ceiling
- Nitrile gloves

---

## Order of operations, in one line

**Acclimate → set divider to the dry plank stack → fix divider → floor → back (full) →
sides (trim back end ¼") → ceiling (trim + props) → leave bare, off-gas 1–2 weeks.**
