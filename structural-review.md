# Structural Review — Climate Guitar Cabinet

**Reviewer:** structural / furniture engineer.
**Scope:** structure, stability, weight limits, risks of the as-built cabinet
(outer 101 × 47.5 × 194 cm; ¾ in Baltic-birch carcass, ½ in back; sealed cedar
vault over an open amp zone; tempered-glass cherry door; floor guitar rack).
**Note:** the box is built, so every fix below is a **retrofit**.

## Verdict
As a *cabinet* it's sound. As a *structure* it is **tall, shallow, heavy, and
top-openable** — loaded ≈ **160 kg (≈ 350 lb)** with a height-to-depth ratio of
**~4 : 1**. Two risks govern everything else:

1. **Tip-over** — the safety risk. Fix before loading anything.
2. **Long-term sag of the divider shelf** under the vault load.

Both are cheap to retrofit. Nothing here is a "don't build it" — it's "anchor it
and stiffen two things."

| Item | kg | lb |
|---|---|---|

## Findings (ranked by risk)

### 1 · Tip-over — **CRITICAL**
**What:** 194 cm tall on a 47.5 cm-deep footprint. Static tip angle ≈ **16°**.
Worst case: the **glass door open** (~18 kg swung forward) while you lean in to pull
a guitar from the floor rack.
**Why:** tall + shallow + a heavy door swinging forward pushes the center of gravity
toward/over the front feet. A lean, a child, a pet, or a door-open reach can topple
~160 kg — a serious injury and total-loss event.
**How to fix:**
- **Anchor to the wall — mandatory, non-negotiable.** Two anti-tip straps or L-brackets
  from the **top rail into wall studs** (real lumber, not drywall anchors alone).
- Keep the **heaviest items (amps) at the bottom** — they already are; don't move them up.
- Optional margin: deepen the base front-to-back by ~3–5 cm, or add a small rear
  anti-tip foot.
- **Don't open the door fully until the wall anchor is in.**

### 2 · Divider-shelf sag — **HIGH**
**What:** the ¾ in plywood divider spans **~97 cm** and carries the **entire vault
load (~45 kg)** — rack, ~7 guitars, climate gear.
**Why:** a ¾ in ply shelf at 38 in span deflects ~2 mm immediately, and plywood
**creeps** under constant load → **~4–6 mm over a few years**. That crowns the vault
floor, pre-stresses the sealed joints, and looks saggy.
**How to fix:** add a **stiffener rail** — glue + screw a hardwood/ply strip
(~40 × 20 mm) under the **front edge** of the divider (and ideally the back), running
side to side, its ends landing on **cleats** screwed to the side panels. That converts
the shelf into a supported beam and the deflection becomes negligible. (A single
center post to the bottom panel also works.)

### 3 · Divider-to-side joints — **HIGH**
**What:** the vault load passes through the divider's **end joints** into the sides in shear.
**Why:** if those joints are glue-only or screwed into plywood **edge grain**, constant
shear + creep can slowly open them (edge-grain screws pull out weakly).
**How to fix:** the **cleats/brackets from #2 carry this load**. Add an L-bracket or
glued block under the divider at **each end** so the shelf bears mechanically on the
sides, not on glue alone.

### 4 · Racking / staying square — **HIGH**
**What:** a tall case wants to **rack** (deform into a parallelogram); the ½ in **back
panel** is the main element resisting it.
**Why:** if the back isn't fully fastened, the box flexes, the **door stops sealing**,
and the wall anchor ends up doing the work.
**How to fix:** make sure the **back is glued and screwed/stapled every 10–15 cm around
the full perimeter and into the divider**. Add two corner gussets at the top if it
feels at all loose.

### 5 · Heavy glass door — **HIGH**
**What:** the door (~13 kg glass + ~5 kg frame ≈ **18 kg**) hangs off the hinge stile
and swings forward.
**Why:** a heavy tall door **racks the case toward the hinge side**, sags over time,
and its swing is the main **tip trigger** (#1). Hinge screws in a weak stile can pull out.
**How to fix:**
- Use the **continuous piano hinge** (spreads the load — already specced).
- Fasten the hinge into the **cherry stile AND the plywood side behind it** with long screws.
- Keep the glass **as light as the size allows** (3/16 in / 5 mm tempered beats ¼ in).
- Add a **stop or soft-close** so it can't fly open.

### 6 · Base / plinth + levelers — **MEDIUM**
**What:** the whole 160 kg lands on the ~14 cm base through leveling feet.
**Why:** a tall load on a weak/spindly base racks; under-rated feet crush or dent the floor.
**How to fix:** base = a **rigid closed box** (not legs); levelers **rated > 250 kg
total** with wide pads, all bearing evenly; **level it** so the door seals and the
divider isn't pre-stressed.

### 7 · Handling / sustained load — **MEDIUM**
**What:** ~160 kg piece; a tall one.
**Why:** the **most likely failure moment is a move** — tipping while carrying it loaded.
**How to fix:** move it **empty and upright**, 2–3 people with straps; **anchor before
loading**. Expect long-term plywood creep — the stiffener (#2) is what keeps it honest.

---

## Priority action list (do in order)
1. **Anchor to wall studs** — before any load goes in.
2. **Divider stiffener rail + end cleats** (fixes #2 and #3).
3. **Fully fasten the back panel** (racking).
4. **Reinforce the hinge mounting; keep the glass thin; add a door stop.**
5. **Rigid base on rated, level feet.**

None of these change the design — they're add-on reinforcements. With the wall
anchor and the divider stiffener, the cabinet is safe and stable for the long term.
